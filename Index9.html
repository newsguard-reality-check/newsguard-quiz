How to Spot Fake Brand Claims
  • Check the company's own channels. Real McDonald's news — statements, product launches, executive comments — shows up on its official newsroom, social accounts, or in mainstream news coverage. If it only exists on random posts, be skeptical.
  • Look for actual news coverage. Genuine controversies get reported by multiple credible outlets. A "viral" claim with zero real news coverage is a red flag.
  • Watch for satire and parody accounts. Many fake brand stories originate from satire sites or parody accounts whose disclaimers get stripped out as screenshots get reshared.
  • Check the date. Old, resolved, or out-of-context stories often get recirculated as if they're breaking news.
  • Be wary of claims that fit too neatly into a narrative. Stories that seem designed to provoke outrage around a hot-button issue deserve extra scrutiny before you share.
  • Search before you share. A quick search for the claim plus "fact check" often surfaces whether it's already been debunked or confirmed.
